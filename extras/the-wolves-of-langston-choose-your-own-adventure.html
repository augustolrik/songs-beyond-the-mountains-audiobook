# The Wolves of Langston

## A mystery-focused choose-your-own-adventure version

This version is built for exploration. There are no random deaths and no dice rolls. Bad choices can close doors, damage trust, or lead to incomplete answers, but the story is meant to let the reader learn the town, the people, and the mystery.

Keep a simple note sheet while you play:

- Clues found:
- People who trust you:
- Places explored:
- What you think is really happening:

Start at section 1.

---

## 1. The Road into Langston

The road to Langston runs between black pines and pale stones. The town should be visible by now, but the forest seems to fold around you, hiding the lights until you are almost on top of them.

A wolf howls somewhere behind you.

Then another answers from the left.

The sound is not quite a hunting call. It is too measured. Too watchful.

Ahead, lanterns glow through mist. Langston sits in a shallow valley, its roofs pressed close together as if the buildings are trying to keep warm. You can smell wet straw, chimney smoke, and something bitter underneath it all - old herbs, old fear.

A stocky dwarf steps out from between two trees. His red beard is tied with leather cords, and his coat is patched with leaves and burrs.

"Easy now," he says, holding up both hands. "The wolves are loud tonight, but loud is not the same as hungry."

He sneezes hard enough to shake the pine needles above him.

"Name's Roi Sunderhammer. Druid, woodsman, occasional rescuer of lost travelers. You're heading to Langston?"

If you accept Roi's company into town, go to 2.

If you ask why the wolves are behaving strangely, go to 3.

If you walk ahead alone and observe the town before meeting anyone, go to 4.

---

## 2. Roi Walks Beside You

Roi walks with a rolling stride, humming under his breath. Every few steps he glances into the trees, not with fear, but with worry.

"There was a death in town," he says. "Maybelline. Good woman. Too young. Langston is taking it badly."

"The wolves?" you ask.

He looks away before answering.

"Wolves follow sickness sometimes. Not because they caused it. Because the world knows when something is wrong."

The town gate is open, but no guard stands there. A string of prayer ribbons hangs from the arch. Many are fresh. Some are torn.

Roi points toward the largest building beside the square.

"The Pickled Hen. Funeral gathering is there. If you want food, answers, or trouble, that is where you'll find all three."

Before he leaves, Roi presses a small twist of dried mint into your palm.

"If the air tastes bitter, chew that. It helps."

Record the clue: Roi fears sickness more than wolves.

Go to 5.

---

## 3. Questions for the Druid

Roi's expression tightens when you ask about the wolves.

"They are not acting like themselves," he says. "Some avoid town. Some circle it. Some come close, stare at windows, then vanish. That is not hunger."

He crouches beside a muddy rut and points to a paw print. The print is large, but the edge is uneven, as if the animal had staggered.

"Something is moving through the forest and the town both. The wolves feel it. So do I."

He sneezes again. This time his eyes flash gold for half a breath.

Roi notices that you noticed.

"Do not mention that in the tavern," he says quietly. "Langston has enough fear already."

Record the clue: Roi is changing in small ways.

If you promise discretion and walk with him, go to 2.

If you press him about the golden eyes, go to 6.

---

## 4. A Stranger at the Edge

You circle wide before entering Langston.

The town has a smithy, a shrine, a small apothecary, a mill, and a square built around an old stone well. The houses are close, their shutters fastened. Almost every door has a small sun symbol chalked above it.

Near the well, a dog sniffs at the stones, whines, and backs away.

Across the square, a young woman in white cleric robes hurries toward The Pickled Hen with papers clutched to her chest. Her face is calm in the lantern light, but her hands are shaking.

If you follow the cleric into The Pickled Hen, go to 5.

If you inspect the well before joining the funeral, go to 7.

If you visit the apothecary first, go to 8.

---

## 5. The Pickled Hen

The Pickled Hen is too full and too quiet.

People stand shoulder to shoulder around a closed coffin. No one laughs. No one speaks above a whisper. The smell of ale and hot stew is buried beneath candle wax and grief.

A young woman in sunburst robes steps forward.

"May the light hold Maybelline," she says. "May the morning find what night has hidden."

Her voice is steady, but she avoids looking at the coffin.

A tall woman with iron-gray hair stands near the front. Her clothes are plain but expensive, and a trained hound sits perfectly still at her heel. People make space for her without being asked.

That must be Mayor Anathram.

Near the hearth, a handsome man with ink-stained fingers stares at the floor. Several townsfolk glance toward him with sympathy, resentment, or both.

If you listen to the cleric Solaine after the service, go to 9.

If you speak to Mayor Anathram, go to 10.

If you approach the grieving man by the hearth, go to 11.

If you quietly study the room first, go to 12.

---

## 6. The Golden-Eyed Sneeze

Roi stops beneath the gate arch.

"Fine," he says. "You saw it. My eyes change when the fit takes me. My hearing sharpens. Smells become too strong. Meat turns my stomach and then, a moment later, I crave it."

He grips the gatepost until his knuckles pale.

"I am not cursed in the old storybook way. I have not attacked anyone. But something is pushing wolf nature into people, and people nature into wolves. If Langston decides I am the monster, they will stop looking for the truth."

His honesty costs him. You can see it.

Record the clue: the mystery blurs the line between people and wolves.

If you offer to help him quietly, go to 2.

If you decide the mayor should know immediately, go to 10.

---

## 7. The Old Well

The well stones are damp though no rain has fallen.

A sour smell rises from below. Not rot. Not poison exactly. More like crushed green stems left too long in a closed jar.

The dog you saw earlier creeps back, hackles raised. When you lift the bucket, the dog barks once and retreats.

Inside the bucket is clear water with tiny silver threads floating in it. They vanish when the lantern light shifts.

Someone has chalked a sun symbol on the inside of the well wall where most people would never see it.

Record the clue: the well water carries silver threads and a hidden sun mark.

If you take a sample of water and go to the apothecary, go to 8.

If you take this clue to Solaine, go to 9.

If you go to the funeral at The Pickled Hen, go to 5.

---

## 8. Tomlin's Apothecary

The apothecary door is unlocked.

Inside, bundles of herbs hang from the ceiling. Bottles line the shelves in careful rows. A ledger lies open on the counter beside a dried blue flower pressed flat beneath glass.

A note in the ledger repeats three words:

Moonwort. Fever. Howling.

Before you can read more, the handsome man from the funeral enters through the back room. His face is pale.

"You should not be here," he says. Then, softer: "No one should be here."

This is Tomlin. Maybelline's betrothed.

If you apologize and ask about the ledger, go to 13.

If you show him the well water, go to 14.

If you leave before frightening him further, go to 5.

---

## 9. Solaine's Burden

Solaine stands in a narrow hallway behind the tavern room, pressing her palms together until the knuckles whiten.

Up close, she looks younger than she sounded during the service. Exhaustion shadows her eyes.

"If you are here to ask whether Maybelline was killed by wolves, I do not know," she says. "If you are here to ask whether the town wants that answer to be true, then yes."

She lowers her voice.

"A wolf attack is simple. A curse is simple. A guilty person is simple. Langston is begging for simple."

If you tell her about the well, go to 15.

If you ask why she avoided looking at the coffin, go to 16.

If you ask who benefits from a simple answer, go to 17.

---

## 10. Mayor Anathram

Mayor Anathram studies you as if you are a tool she has not decided whether to use.

"Langston does not need panic," she says. "It needs order."

Her hound watches your hands.

"Maybelline is dead. Wolves have grown bold. A druid is behaving strangely. A cleric is frightened. A grieving man has secrets. You see the problem."

"Which problem?" you ask.

"Too many stories," the mayor says. "A town can survive danger. It cannot survive everyone choosing a different truth."

If you offer to investigate before anyone is blamed, go to 18.

If you tell her Roi may be changing, go to 19.

If you challenge her need for control, go to 20.

---

## 11. Tomlin by the Hearth

Tomlin holds a cup of wine he has not touched.

"She hated funerals," he says before you ask anything. "Maybelline said people speak better of the dead because they are frightened the dead might hear the truth."

He gives a broken laugh.

"She would have hated this one most of all."

When someone mentions wolves nearby, Tomlin flinches.

If you ask about Maybelline's last days, go to 21.

If you ask why people seem uneasy around him, go to 22.

If you leave him to grieve and observe others, go to 12.

---

## 12. What the Room Reveals

You study the funeral gathering without interrupting it.

Three things stand out.

First, people fear the forest, but they keep looking toward the town well.

Second, several townsfolk smile sadly at Tomlin, while others watch him like a locked door.

Third, Mayor Anathram watches Solaine, not the coffin.

Roi enters late, sneezes, and every dog in the room growls.

Record the clue: the town's fear points inward as much as outward.

If you follow Roi outside, go to 23.

If you speak to Solaine, go to 9.

If you speak to Tomlin, go to 11.

If you speak to the mayor, go to 10.

---

## 13. The Ledger

Tomlin closes the ledger, then opens it again.

"Maybelline came to me with a fever," he says. "Not a normal fever. She heard howling when no wolf was near. She smelled rain before clouds formed. She said the well tasted like metal."

He touches the pressed blue flower.

"Moonwort grows near old ruins north of town. It can calm the symptoms. I made a tonic. It helped, then it didn't."

"Why hide this?"

His face tightens.

"Because if people learn she was changing, they will remember her as a monster instead of Maybelline."

Record the clue: Maybelline had the same signs Roi has now.

If you ask who else knew, go to 24.

If you ask to see the tonic, go to 25.

If you bring Tomlin to Solaine, go to 26.

---

## 14. Water on the Counter

Tomlin stares at the sample from the well.

"Where did you get that?"

When you answer, he takes a glass rod and stirs the water. The silver threads gather toward the rod like iron filings to a magnet.

"This is not poison," he says. "It is a carrier. Something has been steeped in the well to spread slowly. Small doses. Repeated exposure."

He looks toward the door.

"Maybelline drank more well water than anyone. She delivered bread, checked on elders, helped at the shrine. She was always thirsty in summer."

Record the clue: the well is spreading the change slowly.

If you ask Tomlin who could reach the well unseen, go to 24.

If you take this to the mayor, go to 27.

If you take this to Solaine, go to 15.

---

## 15. Solaine and the Hidden Mark

When you mention the chalked sun mark inside the well, Solaine goes still.

"That is not mine," she says.

She draws the symbol she uses for morning rites: a round sun with eight even rays. Then she draws what you saw: a sun split by a crooked line.

"That mark belongs to an older local practice. Not evil. Not exactly. Langston used it generations ago when the town believed the forest had to be bargained with."

"Bargained how?"

"Blood, water, and promises."

Record the clue: the hidden sun mark is older than Solaine's faith.

If you ask where old records are kept, go to 28.

If you ask whether Maybelline knew about this, go to 29.

If you bring Solaine to the well, go to 30.

---

## 16. The Coffin Question

Solaine looks toward the tavern room.

"I did not look because I blessed the body this morning," she says. "Maybelline's hands were stained with soil under the nails. Her feet were clean. That means she clawed at earth after death, or someone wanted it to look that way."

She swallows.

"I told the mayor. She told me grief makes signs where there are none."

Record the clue: Maybelline's body may have been moved or staged.

If you ask to examine where Maybelline was found, go to 31.

If you take this to Mayor Anathram, go to 20.

If you ask Solaine to trust Tomlin with this, go to 26.

---

## 17. Who Wants a Simple Answer?

Solaine thinks carefully.

"The mayor wants calm. Roi wants the wolves spared. Tomlin wants Maybelline remembered kindly. The townsfolk want permission to be afraid."

"And you?"

"I want the truth before fear chooses a victim."

She gives you a small iron key.

"The shrine records are in the back room. If this began before Maybelline, the old death notices may show it."

Record the clue: Solaine gives you access to the shrine records.

Go to 28.

---

## 18. The Mayor's Terms

The mayor nods.

"Investigate, then. But do it cleanly. No shouting in the square. No accusations without proof. If the town panics, people will run into the forest, and then wolves will not be the worst danger."

She gives you three official paths:

"The well. The old records. The place where Maybelline was found."

Then she adds, almost unwillingly:

"And if Roi loses control, send him to me before the town sees."

Record the clue: the mayor knows the main places to investigate.

If you inspect the well, go to 7.

If you inspect shrine records, go to 28.

If you inspect where Maybelline was found, go to 31.

---

## 19. Accusing Roi Too Early

The mayor listens without expression as you describe Roi's changing eyes and senses.

"Useful," she says.

Within minutes, two militia volunteers leave the inn.

You find Roi near the stable, surrounded but not attacked. His face shows hurt more than fear when he sees you.

"You could have asked me to stand before her," he says. "Instead you handed her a rumor."

The mayor does not imprison him, but Roi no longer trusts you alone with his secrets.

Record the consequence: Roi's trust is damaged.

If you apologize and ask what he knows anyway, go to 23.

If you leave him and follow the official investigation, go to 18.

---

## 20. The Mayor Under Pressure

When you challenge the mayor, the hound rises.

Anathram lifts one hand, and the dog sits again.

"You think control is vanity," she says. "It is not. I have seen frightened people become mobs. I have seen mobs choose the nearest person who looks different and call that justice."

For the first time, her voice cracks.

"If I seem cold, it is because warmth will not stop Langston from tearing itself open."

She is not innocent of pressure, but she is not careless either.

Record the clue: the mayor is hiding fear, not just authority.

If you ask what she has withheld, go to 32.

If you ask for permission to gather everyone later, go to 33.

If you leave to collect proof first, go to 18.

---

## 21. Maybelline's Last Days

Tomlin speaks slowly, choosing each memory like a fragile cup.

"She became restless. She walked at night. She said the forest had started speaking in patterns. Not words. Patterns."

"Was she afraid?"

"At first, no. She thought something needed help. Then she found old stones north of town and came back shaking."

He looks toward the coffin.

"The next day she ended our engagement. Said she loved me too much to let me follow."

Record the clue: Maybelline found old stones north of town.

If you ask Tomlin to show you the stones, go to 34.

If you compare this with Roi's knowledge of the forest, go to 23.

If you investigate records first, go to 28.

---

## 22. Tomlin's Reputation

Tomlin laughs without humor.

"I am useful, charming, and too good at knowing what people want. That makes half the town fond of me and half the town suspicious."

He looks toward a table of older women.

"Maybelline trusted me. That should be enough. But grief makes people hunt for flaws."

When he turns, you notice a scratch on his wrist. Not a claw mark. A thorn scratch.

Record the clue: Tomlin has been near thorny growth recently.

If you ask about the scratch, go to 35.

If you ask about Maybelline's illness, go to 13.

---

## 23. Roi at the Stable

Roi stands outside the stable, breathing through the mint he gave you.

"The animals smell it too," he says. "Whatever is in the well, it does not stop at people."

He gestures toward the hills north of town.

"There are old stones out there. Before Langston had walls, it had bargains. People promised the forest respect. The forest promised warning. Somewhere along the way, someone decided warning was not enough."

If Roi trusts you, he adds:

"I heard Maybelline singing near the stones the night before she died. Not happy singing. Remembering singing."

Record the clue: Maybelline visited the old stones before she died.

If you ask Roi to guide you to the stones, go to 34.

If you ask him how to calm the wolves, go to 36.

If you return to town for more proof first, go to 18.

---

## 24. Who Else Knew?

Tomlin counts on his fingers.

"Me. Solaine. Maybe Roi, though Maybelline did not want him blamed. The mayor suspected. Maybelline's grandmother would have known, but she died years ago."

He hesitates.

"There is one more thing. Maybelline kept a box under a loose floorboard in her room. Letters, old charms, childhood treasures. I could not bring myself to open it."

Record the clue: Maybelline left a hidden box.

If you ask Tomlin to take you to Maybelline's room, go to 37.

If you bring this information to Solaine, go to 26.

---

## 25. The Tonic

Tomlin gives you a small bottle of blue-green liquid.

"Moonwort, mint, charcoal, and prayer," he says. "The last ingredient was Solaine's."

The tonic smells clean at first, then sharply bitter. It does not cure the change, but it seems to slow it.

If you have Roi's trust, you may later use this to help him stay calm.

Record the clue: moonwort slows the symptoms.

If you go to Roi, go to 23.

If you go to Solaine, go to 26.

If you go to the old stones, go to 34.

---

## 26. Three Witnesses

You bring Tomlin and Solaine together in the shrine.

At first they speak like people stepping around broken glass. Then Maybelline's name softens them both.

Tomlin shares the ledger. Solaine shares what she saw on the body. Together they find a pattern:

Maybelline was not the first to hear howling.

Two elders reported it before they died of "winter fever." A miller's child had it and recovered after drinking only rainwater for a week. Roi's symptoms began after he blessed the well with the old seasonal rite.

Record the clue: the well has been carrying the change for longer than anyone admitted.

If you inspect the shrine records, go to 28.

If you gather the mayor too, go to 33.

If you go to the old stones with this group, go to 34.

---

## 27. The Mayor and the Water

Mayor Anathram does not dismiss the sample.

She calls her hound close, opens the bottle, and lets the dog sniff. The hound whines and hides behind her chair.

For a moment, the mayor looks old.

"I had the well watched," she says. "No stranger touched it. No enemy crept in."

"Then someone inside Langston did."

"Or something underneath it."

Record the clue: the mayor's watch saw no outside culprit.

If you ask what she has withheld, go to 32.

If you ask to question the well guards, go to 38.

If you take the water to Solaine, go to 15.

---

## 28. Shrine Records

The shrine back room smells of dust and lamp oil.

Solaine's key opens a cabinet full of birth records, death notices, and old festival songs. The recent pages are neat. The older pages are written in several hands.

One line repeats every generation:

When the wolves sing at the well, Langston must answer before the moon is full.

A later mayor crossed out the line and wrote:

Old fear. No civic value.

In the oldest book, you find a map to the standing stones north of town. A note beside it says:

The spring below the stones feeds the well. Keep the spring clean. Keep the promise honest.

Record the clue: the old stones feed the town well.

If you go to the standing stones, go to 34.

If you take the records to the mayor, go to 32.

If you take the records to Solaine and Tomlin, go to 26.

---

## 29. Maybelline's Private Faith

Solaine smiles sadly.

"Maybelline helped at the shrine, but she also kept old customs. Ribbons on branches. Bread at the forest edge. Songs her grandmother taught her."

"Did that bother you?"

"No. Faith is not made smaller by memory."

Solaine pauses.

"But the mayor disliked those customs. She thought they made Langston look backward."

Record the clue: Maybelline knew old forest customs the mayor disliked.

If you search Maybelline's room, go to 37.

If you ask the mayor about old customs, go to 20.

If you go to the records, go to 28.

---

## 30. At the Well with Solaine

Solaine lowers a lantern into the well.

The hidden sun mark seems to move in the light. Not magically. It has been redrawn many times, one chalk line over another.

"Someone has renewed this mark," she says.

The bucket rope is stained with blue pollen.

Moonwort.

Record the clue: moonwort pollen is on the well rope.

If you compare this with Tomlin's tonic, go to 14.

If you ask who handles the rope daily, go to 38.

If you follow the water source north, go to 34.

---

## 31. Where Maybelline Was Found

The place lies beyond the mill, where town grass becomes forest fern.

There is no blood on the ground. No torn cloth. No signs of a wolf pack dragging prey.

There are, however, two sets of footprints: Maybelline's light steps moving toward town, and a heavier set moving beside her. The heavier steps turn back toward the old stones.

You also find a scrap of ribbon tied to a thorn bush. It is faded yellow, the color of morning rites and old forest promises.

Record the clue: Maybelline was likely walking with someone, not fleeing wolves.

If you follow the heavier tracks, go to 34.

If you compare the ribbon with shrine records, go to 28.

If you ask Tomlin about thorn scratches, go to 35.

---

## 32. What the Mayor Withheld

The mayor closes the door before speaking.

"My grandmother believed in the old bargain. She said the spring under the stones could carry warnings into blood. Sharpen senses. Stir wolf-dreams. Help Langston notice danger before danger arrived."

"And now?"

"Now the warning has become sickness."

She opens a drawer and removes a bundle of old ribbons.

"Maybelline asked me to restore the old rite. I refused. She went without permission."

The mayor's jaw tightens.

"I thought she was being sentimental. Then she died."

Record the clue: Maybelline tried to restore an old protective rite.

If you ask the mayor to join a public truth-telling, go to 33.

If you go to the old stones, go to 34.

If you investigate the well guards first, go to 38.

---

## 33. Gathering the Town

You ask the key people to meet in the tavern before fear chooses its own culprit.

The result depends on what you have learned.

If you have at least four clues, including one about the well or old stones, go to 39.

If you have fewer than four clues, go to 40.

---

## 34. The Standing Stones

The path north climbs through pine and wet bracken.

The standing stones wait in a ring around a spring. Ribbons hang from branches nearby: old ones faded white, new ones yellow, one dark with mud.

The spring water glitters with the same silver threads you saw in the well.

At the center of the stones lies a cracked clay vessel packed with moonwort, hair, ash, and a strip of cloth from Maybelline's dress.

No random beast did this.

This is a failed ritual, or a ritual interrupted.

A low growl rises from the trees. Wolves step into view, but they do not attack. They form a wide circle, watching the spring.

If Roi is with you or trusts you, go to 41.

If Solaine and Tomlin are with you, go to 42.

If you came alone, go to 43.

---

## 35. The Thorn Scratch

Tomlin looks at his wrist.

"I went after her," he says. "Not that night. The next morning. I found the stones and the broken vessel. I cut myself searching the thorns for anything she left behind."

"Why hide it?"

"Because I panicked. Because I thought if people learned about ritual herbs and old blood promises, they would blame her. Or Solaine. Or Roi. Anyone except the truth."

"What is the truth?"

Tomlin looks away.

"Maybelline was trying to heal the town."

Record the clue: Tomlin found the broken ritual after Maybelline died.

If you ask him to guide you there, go to 34.

If you bring him to Solaine, go to 26.

---

## 36. Calming the Wolves

Roi teaches you how to stand where wolves can see your hands and hear your breath.

"They do not want a fight," he says. "They want the wrongness gone."

He gives you three instructions:

"Do not run. Do not bare your teeth. Do not pretend you are not afraid. Wolves understand honest fear."

Record the clue: the wolves are witnesses, not villains.

If you go to the standing stones with Roi, go to 41.

If you gather others first, go to 33.

---

## 37. Maybelline's Room

Maybelline's room is small, clean, and full of practical kindness: mended gloves, spare candles, folded letters, a list of neighbors who needed help.

Under a loose floorboard you find a wooden box.

Inside are yellow ribbons, a child's drawing of wolves around a sun, and a letter addressed to "Whoever comes after me."

The letter reads:

I thought the old bargain was a story. It is not. The spring warns us when the forest is sick, but we stopped listening. I tried to renew the promise alone. That was pride. If I fail, do not hunt the wolves. Find the spring. Clean what we made bitter. Tell Tomlin I was not leaving him. I was trying to come back whole.

Record the clue: Maybelline tried to save Langston and knew she might fail.

If you bring the letter to Tomlin and Solaine, go to 26.

If you bring the letter to the mayor, go to 32.

If you go to the stones, go to 34.

---

## 38. The Well Watch

The well guards are not soldiers. They are tired townsfolk with lanterns and short clubs.

They swear no stranger came near the well. But one old woman remembers seeing blue pollen on the rope after the mayor's seasonal inspection. A young man remembers Tomlin drawing water at dawn. A miller remembers Solaine praying there after midnight.

The stories contradict each other until you ask one different question:

"Who had reason to hide what they were doing?"

The answer is everyone.

Record the clue: many people touched the well for protective reasons, but no one coordinated.

If you gather everyone, go to 33.

If you go to the old stones, go to 34.

---

## 39. The Town Hears the Pattern

You speak carefully.

You do not accuse one person. You lay out the pattern: the spring feeds the well, the old warning rite was forgotten, Maybelline tried to renew it alone, Tomlin hid her symptoms out of love, Solaine hid her doubts out of fear of panic, Roi hid his changes to avoid becoming a scapegoat, and the mayor hid old knowledge because she thought order required silence.

The room resists the truth at first. Then Maybelline's letter, the water sample, the records, or the standing-stone evidence gives the truth weight.

The town has not been attacked by wolves.

It has been warned by them.

If you lead the town to repair the spring together, go to 44.

If you place the work in the mayor's hands, go to 45.

If you ask Solaine, Roi, and Tomlin to lead a quieter solution, go to 46.

---

## 40. Too Soon

You gather the town before you have enough proof.

Fear fills the gaps.

Some blame Roi. Some blame Tomlin. Some blame Solaine's foreign rites. The mayor restores order, but only by ending the meeting and sending everyone home.

No one dies. No mob forms. But trust becomes harder.

You can still finish the mystery, but you need proof.

Choose one place you have not explored:

The well, go to 7.

The records, go to 28.

Maybelline's room, go to 37.

The old stones, go to 34.

---

## 41. Roi in the Wolf Circle

Roi steps into the ring of wolves.

His shoulders tremble. His eyes flash gold. For a moment, you think he may fall to all fours.

If you have Tomlin's tonic, you give it to him. If not, you remind him of his own words: honest fear, steady breath.

The largest wolf approaches Roi and touches its nose to his hand.

Roi begins to weep.

"They are sick too," he says. "Not cursed. Sick."

Together you remove the cracked vessel from the spring. The silver threads thin, but they do not vanish.

Record the clue: removing the vessel helps but does not fully heal the spring.

If you return with this evidence to town, go to 39.

If you try to complete Maybelline's rite with Roi, go to 47.

---

## 42. Solaine, Tomlin, and the Broken Rite

Solaine kneels by the spring while Tomlin examines the vessel.

"The ingredients are wrong," he says. "Too much moonwort. No charcoal. No clean water. Whoever copied the rite copied symptoms, not meaning."

Solaine studies the ribbons.

"Maybelline did not do all this. She knew songs and promises. This vessel is made like a recipe."

Tomlin's face changes.

"My ledger."

Someone used his notes without understanding them.

Record the clue: someone copied Tomlin's treatment notes into a broken ritual.

If you return to town with this evidence, go to 39.

If you search around the stones for the copyist, go to 48.

---

## 43. Alone at the Stones

Alone, you can see the shape of the mystery, but not hold all of it.

The wolves watch. The spring glitters. The broken vessel smells of bitter herbs.

You can remove the vessel, and the water clears a little. But without witnesses, the town may not believe what you found. Without the old songs, you may not know how to mend what was broken.

You take the vessel and return safely.

Record the clue: the spring was contaminated by a broken ritual vessel.

If you bring the vessel to Solaine, go to 26.

If you bring the vessel to the mayor, go to 32.

If you bring the vessel to Roi, go to 23.

## 44. Ending - The Town Keeps the Promise

Langston walks to the stones together.

Not everyone. Some are too frightened, too angry, or too ashamed. But enough come.

Solaine speaks a morning prayer and leaves space for the older song. Roi guides the wolves back with open hands. Tomlin prepares clean charcoal and moonwort in the proper measure. Mayor Anathram removes her gloves and helps scrub the spring stones herself.

No single person saves Langston.

That is the point.

By dawn, the silver threads are gone from the water. The wolves remain at the forest edge until the first sunlight touches the well, then vanish between the pines.

Maybelline is remembered not as a victim of wolves, but as the first person brave enough to hear the warning.

This is the best ending: truth shared, trust repaired, and the mystery understood.

---

## 45. Ending - Order Restored, Questions Buried

You place the evidence in Mayor Anathram's hands.

She acts quickly. The well is closed. The spring is cleaned by a small trusted group. Roi is protected from accusation. Tomlin is told to burn the dangerous notes. Solaine is asked to write an official account.

Langston survives.

But the town does not fully learn what happened. People know enough to stop drinking from the well for a time, but not enough to understand why silence nearly destroyed them.

Weeks later, the wolves stop circling the town. Life returns to normal.

Almost.

This is a stable ending: the danger passes, but the deeper lesson remains unfinished.

---

## 46. Ending - The Quiet Cure

You decide the whole town is not ready for the truth.

Solaine, Roi, and Tomlin work through the night. They clean the spring, burn the broken vessel, and mark the well with both the new sun and the old promise. The mayor learns enough to keep people calm, but not enough to control the story.

By morning, the water clears.

The wolves leave.

Tomlin reads Maybelline's letter alone at the stones. Roi sits beside him until he finishes. Solaine sings the old song softly, not as doctrine, but as memory.

Langston is safe, though still divided.

This is a compassionate ending: the mystery is solved by a few, but the town may need years to become honest with itself.

---

## 47. Ending - The Druid's Pact

You and Roi complete the rite without waiting for the town.

It works, but it costs him.

Roi takes the warning into himself. The spring clears. The well tastes clean. The wolves bow their heads and retreat into the forest.

Roi remains human, but changed. His eyes stay gold. His senses never fully dull. He becomes Langston's living warning bell, loved by some, feared by others.

He does not regret it.

"Better me by choice," he says, "than the whole town by accident."

This is a heroic ending: Langston is saved, but the burden falls too heavily on one person.

## 48. The Copyist's Trail

Behind the stones, you find a scrap of paper sealed in wax.

It is not a villain's confession. It is worse in a quieter way: a frightened neighbor copying Tomlin's notes, Solaine's prayers, and old festival lines into one desperate ritual.

The handwriting belongs to Edda Vale, the miller's sister. Her child once recovered after drinking rainwater. She must have thought the old rite could protect everyone if Maybelline would not act fast enough.

When confronted, Edda does not run.

"I only wanted the warning to be strong," she says. "Strong enough that no one could ignore it."

If you bring Edda before the town with the full story, go to 49.

If you hide her role and focus on healing the spring, go to 46.

If you let the mayor judge her quietly, go to 45.

---

## 49. Ending - Mercy with Accountability

You bring Edda to the tavern, but you do not let the room turn her into the whole answer.

She did wrong. She broke the rite, contaminated the spring, and hid when Maybelline died.

But she did not create the town's silence. She did not make Tomlin hide illness, Solaine swallow doubt, Roi fear accusation, or the mayor bury old knowledge.

Langston hears the hard version of the truth.

Edda is not exiled. She is made to help clean the spring, care for the sick, and speak Maybelline's warning at the next festival. The punishment is public work, not revenge.

The wolves leave after dawn.

Maybelline's letter is copied into the shrine records, beneath the old line:

When the wolves sing at the well, Langston must answer before the moon is full.

This is the justice ending: the culprit is named, but the town also accepts its share of responsibility.
