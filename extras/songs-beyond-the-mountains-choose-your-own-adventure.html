Songs Beyond the Mountains - Choose Your Own Adventure

The Echo Road

A branching mystery in the setting of Songs Beyond the Mountains.

This is a reading-only choose-your-own-adventure. There is no audio here. You are choosing how to move through a political mystery between Rivergate, Kettlehome, the Black Banner, Vaerith, and the echo-iron beneath the mountains.

There are no random instant deaths. Bad choices can cost trust, lose evidence, strengthen a faction, or lead to an incomplete ending.

Keep a note sheet:

  • Clues found:
  • People who trust you:
  • Factions angered:
  • What you think echo-iron is being used for:

Start at section 1.

1. Rain on the East Road

Rain turns the road east of Rivergate into a gray ribbon of mud. A wagon blocks the way ahead. Its canvas has burned away, but the axle cuts are too clean for accident or ordinary banditry.

A Rivergate patrolman lies beside the ditch, alive but bleeding. He grips a strip of black cloth painted with a white hand across the mouth.

Beyond the road, boot tracks vanish toward the hills.

If you help the wounded patrolman first, go to 2.

If you inspect the wagon and tracks, go to 3.

If you follow the tracks immediately, go to 4.

2. The Patrolman's Name

The patrolman gives his name as Edric Vale.

"They wanted us to see the cloth," he says. "One attacker shouted in Goblin. Badly. Like a market actor. Then someone else told him to shut up. That voice knew command."

Record the clue: one attacker faked Goblin badly, but another gave disciplined orders.

If you take Edric to Rivergate, go to 5.

If you stabilize him and inspect the scene, go to 3.

3. The Wrong Knot

The black cloth is dramatic, but the small things are more useful.

A cord tied around the wagon tongue uses copper-thread made to resemble Kettlehome work. The knot is wrong. Kettlehome ties wet-load knots clockwise so the twist holds in rain. This one is tied counterclockwise.

The wagon tracks are too wide for hill carts. They match heavy Rivergate freight wagons.

Record the clue: the goblin sign is staged, and the wagon tracks point toward Rivergate freight.

If you carry this evidence to Rivergate, go to 5.

If you follow the tracks, go to 4.

If you search the burned cargo, go to 6.

4. The Trail into the Pines

The tracks climb into pine forest. After half a mile, they split.

One path is careless: broken ferns, deep prints, a smear of blood on bark. The other is careful: shallow prints, dragged branches, and one hard wheel rut where no cart should have gone.

If you follow the wounded trail, go to 7.

If you follow the careful trail, go to 8.

If you return to Rivergate before rain erases the road evidence, go to 5.

5. At Rivergate's Gate

Rivergate rises from the rain with gray walls, red roofs, and too many people waiting outside the eastern gate.

Sergeant Nessa steps forward. Her watch cord has knots tied into it, each one small and deliberate.

"Tell me why I should not close this gate and let fear do the rest," she says.

If you present Edric as a living witness, go to 9.

If you present the staged knot and freight tracks, go to 10.

If you accuse Rivergate merchants without proof, go to 11.

6. Cargo That Should Not Be There

Most cargo burned, but rain saved a crate label under the wagon bed.

It says: broken agricultural tools.

The weight marks and iron fittings suggest pump parts, ore carts, lifting tackle, and reinforced cable. Mining equipment.

Someone is moving mining gear through a road they claim is too dangerous for trade.

Record the clue: hidden mining equipment was disguised as broken farm tools.

If you take the label to Rivergate customs, go to 12.

If you follow the careful trail, go to 8.

7. The Wounded Raider

You find a young goblin under a fallen pine, one hand pressed to his side. A white hand is painted badly across his mouth.

"No children," he whispers. "Ruk said no children. Merrow's people broke the terms. Tell him before he makes it worse."

Record the clue: Ruk leads part of the Black Banner, but Merrow broke agreed limits.

If you try to save him, go to 13.

If you search for Ruk's camp, go to 14.

8. The Hidden Loading Hollow

The careful trail leads to a hidden loading hollow. A heavy wagon turned here, unloaded something, and returned toward Rivergate by a different track.

In the mud you find a pearl button with black thread still caught in two holes.

A scrap of paper reads: east quay - unencumbered guarantor - no public ledger.

Record the clue: a black-gloved Rivergate patron or agent used the hidden hollow.

If you go to Rivergate's east quay, go to 15.

If you take the button and note to the council, go to 16.

If you follow the hills deeper, go to 17.

9. Edric Speaks

Edric cannot stand without help, but he refuses to be carried silently.

"I heard bad Goblin and good command," he tells Nessa. "Do not hang a war on a performance."

Nessa orders the gate opened only wide enough for you, Edric, and the evidence.

Record the trust: Nessa believes you are useful.

Go to 18.

10. Evidence Before Accusation

You lay out the wrong knot, freight tracks, and staged cloth.

Nessa listens without blinking.

"A planted clue does not prove innocence," she says. "But it proves someone wants haste. I dislike being hurried by criminals."

Record the trust: Rivergate's watch will not dismiss the staged evidence.

Go to 18.

11. The Gate Turns Cold

You accuse Rivergate merchants before showing proof.

The crowd hears accusation, not evidence. Nessa does not arrest you, but she does not protect you either.

"Bring facts," she says. "Not smoke. We have enough smoke."

Record the consequence: Rivergate trust is damaged.

If you calm down and present evidence, go to 10.

If you leave for the east quay, go to 15.

12. Customs Copies

The customs office smells of wet wool and ink. A clerk named Iven opens the ledgers with shaking precision.

Three shipments of "broken agricultural tools" match the weight of mining gear. Each copy has three binding pins.

"Lady Merrow Vale's countinghouse uses four pins," Iven says. "These are copies of copies. Someone wanted distance."

Record the clue: Merrow's network hides mining equipment through shell records.

If you ask for warehouse locations, go to 15.

If you take this to the council, go to 16.

13. Safe Custody

You bind the wounded goblin's side and arrange for him to be treated as a witness, not a trophy.

His name is Tikk. He says the Black Banner was promised tools, papers, and land near Stonecross. Ruk believed the raids would frighten Rivergate into bargaining, not burn drivers alive.

"Then the silver lady changed the song," Tikk says. "And some of us kept marching because turning back meant admitting the dead bought nothing."

Record the clue: the Black Banner is divided between survival, pride, and revenge.

If you go to the Banner camp, go to 14.

If you take Tikk's testimony to the council, go to 16.

14. The Banner Camp

The camp is not only fighters. You see cooking fires, patched tents, children carrying water, elders drying socks, and archers watching from trees.

A goblin with a square iron cap on one horn steps onto a flat stone bridge.

"I am Ruk," he says. "If Rivergate sent you to count enemies, count the children too. They eat more than arrows."

If you tell Ruk that Tikk says Merrow broke the terms, go to 19.

If you ask what he wants before judging him, go to 20.

If you call him a murderer before hearing the camp, go to 21.

15. East Quay Warehouses

Rivergate's east quay smells of rope, river mud, and money.

Merrow's warehouses are neat enough to feel innocent. That is the first suspicious thing. The second is a locked lower room where no cargo is listed.

Inside, you find ore-cart wheels, pump pieces, black wool, white paint, and a tuning fork wrapped in velvet. When struck, the fork makes the floor answer in the same tone.

Record the clue: Merrow's warehouse connects mining gear, staged Banner cloth, and resonance tools.

If you take the evidence to Duchess Elira, go to 16.

If you follow the resonance below, go to 22.

If you confront Merrow alone, go to 23.

16. The Council Chamber

Duchess Elira listens while older councilors explain fear to her as if she has not noticed it.

Lady Merrow Vale sits on the merchant bench in black gloves with pearl buttons. When you mention mining gear, the lowest button opens and closes beneath her thumb.

Merrow says, "Speculation is not justice. Rivergate cannot gamble its children on a stranger's pattern of mud and thread."

If you have three or more clues, go to 24.

If you have fewer than three clues, go to 25.

If you reveal Kettlehome before its people choose to be known, go to 26.

17. Toward the Hidden Valley

The hills tighten around you. Old maps become less useful. A bridge you saw from the ridge is gone when you reach the stream. Ravens with copper-green necks watch from branches.

At dusk, a goblin woman with three copper rings in one ear steps from the ferns.

"You are close enough to be rude," she says. "I am Sella Hearthstone. Tell me whether you came by accident, greed, or Vaerith's terrible sense of timing."

If you show respect and ask for Kettlehome's side, go to 27.

If you demand to see Vaerith, go to 28.

If you warn her about Merrow's mining gear, go to 29.

18. The Bent Nail

Captain Tern quarters you at the Bent Nail, an inn whose furniture has survived by expecting little from guests.

Pip Thistledown, a halfling courier, slides into the chair opposite you.

"Evidence gets lost unless it knows where to walk," she says. "I know where things walk. Letters. Ledgers. Grief. Bribes. People who do not fit through front doors."

A half-orc musician named Moss listens from the corner, one thumb resting on a viol case.

If you ask Pip about Merrow's warehouses, go to 15.

If you ask Moss about the rhythm of the attacks, go to 30.

If you go to the council now, go to 16.

19. Ruk Hears the Broken Terms

When you tell Ruk what Tikk said, he looks not surprised but confirmed.

"I told her no children," he says. "No burned drivers. Fear, not slaughter. Pressure, not ash."

A hard-eyed archer named Scrip clicks copper beads in her braids.

"You still took her tools," Scrip says. "Do not pretend the song changed without you hearing."

Record the clue: Ruk knew Merrow had crossed lines but stayed because he needed victory.

If you ask Ruk to help expose Merrow, go to 31.

If you ask Mother Tekk what the camp needs, go to 32.

20. What Ruk Wants

"Land," Ruk says. "Papers Rivergate cannot pretend not to read. Wells no one poisons. Law that walks past the wall. A night where children sleep without sentries."

He points toward the hidden valley.

"Kettlehome had safety and called it peace. Vaerith saved one valley and let the rest of us become weather."

Record the clue: the conflict is about land and recognition, not only raids.

If you ask Mother Tekk what peace would require, go to 32.

If you go to Kettlehome with this truth, go to 27.

21. Accusing Ruk

You call Ruk a murderer before hearing the camp.

Some fighters raise bows. Others look ashamed. Mother Tekk, an elder with a black-feather cane, steps between you and the archers.

"Anger is not proof," she says. "Ours or yours."

Ruk lets you leave, but he will not trust you soon.

Record the consequence: Banner trust is damaged.

If you return with proof of Merrow's betrayal, go to 19.

If you leave for Kettlehome, go to 17.

22. Beneath the Warehouse

A stair descends from the warehouse into a brick-lined chamber. In the wall is a dark metal vein. When you speak, it gives your last word back a heartbeat later.

Moss, if she is with you, whispers, "Echo-iron. It remembers pressure in the air. Not meaning. Pressure. That distinction matters."

On a table lie notes about binding names, repeated commands, and a map of mountain caves.

Record the clue: Merrow is using echo-iron to sustain magical commands.

If you take the notes to the College of the Golden Harp, go to 33.

If you take them to the council, go to 24.

23. Tea with Merrow

Merrow receives you in a glass winter garden where bare vines tap the panes like fingernails.

She does not confess. She explains.

"A dragon can burn a town before the second warning bell," she says. "Walls do not matter. Treaties do not matter. Ownership matters. Control matters."

Record the clue: Merrow's fear of dragons drives her plan to own echo-iron and bind Vaerith.

If you take motive plus evidence to the council, go to 24.

If you threaten her, go to 34.

24. Evidence Has Weight

You place the clues in order: staged goblin signs, Merrow freight tracks, hidden mining equipment, shell ledgers, warehouse supplies, echo-iron notes, or Tikk's testimony.

The room changes when the evidence becomes too heavy for convenience.

Duchess Elira orders Merrow's warehouses sealed. Captain Tern assigns guards not connected to merchant houses. Merrow smiles as if law is a room she has furnished before.

Record the progress: Rivergate law is moving, but Merrow is not beaten.

If you rush to protect the College of the Golden Harp, go to 33.

If you warn Kettlehome, go to 29.

If you track Merrow's missing agents, go to 35.

25. Too Little Proof

The council hears you, but fear fills the empty spaces.

Merrow does not need to defeat your story. She only needs to make it sound unfinished.

"Investigate," Duchess Elira says. "But I cannot accuse a house on smoke."

Choose a lead you have not followed:

The east quay, go to 15.

The customs office, go to 12.

The Banner camp, go to 14.

The hidden valley, go to 17.

26. The Cost of Revealing Kettlehome

You reveal Kettlehome before its people choose to be known.

The council erupts. Some want maps. Some want soldiers. Merrow looks almost pleased, because fear has been given a location.

You have not doomed the valley, but you have made consent harder.

Record the consequence: Kettlehome trust is damaged.

If you go to Kettlehome to answer for it, go to 27.

If you repair the damage with evidence against Merrow, go to 24.

27. Sella's Gate

Sella leads you through a gate cut into old stone. Kettlehome opens beyond it in terraces: red roofs, steam from bathhouses, copper chimes, gardens in the rain.

It is not a monster lair. It is a home.

Record the trust: you have seen Kettlehome as a living community.

If you ask the council to open talks with Rivergate, go to 36.

If you ask what Vaerith has hidden, go to 28.

28. Vaerith's Protection

Vaerith of the Verdigris Crown lands on the gatehouse roof hard enough to shake dust from the arch.

He does not deny burning maps, confusing roads, or listening through copper-winged ravens.

"Kettlehome survived," he says.

Sella answers before you can. "Survival is not the same as consent."

Record the clue: Vaerith protected Kettlehome and controlled it.

If you ask about echo-iron, go to 37.

If you bring this truth to the council, go to 36.

29. Warning Kettlehome

You tell Kettlehome that Merrow is moving mining equipment and looking for mountain caves.

The council divides. Some want Vaerith to burn every road west. Others want the sealed passages opened before Merrow finds them.

Sella listens without blinking.

"Safety kept in one fist becomes smaller every year," she says.

Record the progress: Kettlehome knows the danger before Merrow arrives.

If you seek Vaerith's truth, go to 37.

If you ask the council to vote, go to 36.

30. Moss Hears the Pattern

Moss plays three short notes on her viol, then knocks the table twice.

"That is how trained archers call distance," she says. "The attacks were disciplined. But someone wanted witnesses to remember paint, not rhythm."

She looks toward the door.

"Also, echo-iron leaves metal on the tongue when sound passes through it. I tasted it near the bridge."

Record the clue: the attacks and resonance tools are linked by rhythm.

If you go to the bridge site, go to 22.

If you take Moss to the council, go to 16.

31. Ruk's Conditional Help

Ruk will not surrender to Rivergate on hope.

"Bring a public promise," he says. "Land for families who disarm. No bounties. Mixed patrols. Law that names us as people before it names us threats."

Record the option: the Banner can split from Merrow if peace offers material safety.

If you take these terms to Rivergate, go to 38.

If you take them to Kettlehome, go to 36.

32. Mother Tekk Counts Mouths

Mother Tekk shows you the ration slate.

Two hundred and seventeen mouths. Not fighters. Mouths.

"Ruk speaks loudest," she says. "Do not mistake that for alone. If peace feeds no one, revenge will do the talking."

Record the clue: the Banner's future depends on food, land, and legal status.

If you take the ration slate to the council, go to 38.

If you ask Ruk to confront Merrow, go to 31.

33. The College of the Golden Harp

The college smells of varnish, paper, and old arguments.

Master Gongo examines the echo-iron notes and becomes very still.

"A living choir can sustain a binding for a minute," he says. "Echo-iron can remember the pressure of that choir and continue longer. If Merrow learns Vaerith's hidden name, she can make the mountain repeat a chain."

Then the bells in the hall ring without hands.

Merrow has moved first.

If you protect witnesses, go to 39.

If you protect documents, go to 40.

34. Threats in the Garden

You threaten Merrow.

She learns what you know and what you cannot yet prove. That night, one warehouse is emptied, one clerk disappears, and the bridge chamber is scrubbed clean.

Record the consequence: Merrow destroys one evidence trail.

If you still have three clues, go to 24.

If not, choose another lead: customs office go to 12, Banner camp go to 14, Kettlehome go to 17.

35. The Missing Agents

Merrow's missing agents are moving toward the mountains with singers, mining tackle, and sealed contracts.

In an abandoned camp, you find a college hymn sheet with a single name written again and again in the margin.

Vaerith's hidden name.

Record the clue: Merrow is preparing to bind Vaerith in the echo-iron caves.

If you race to Kettlehome, go to 29.

If you gather Rivergate guards first, go to 38.

36. Kettlehome Votes

The council floor is divided by chalk lines.

One side wants Vaerith's old secrecy. One side wants open roads. A third group refuses both choices and demands terms before trust.

You speak of fear and control honestly: Rivergate's fear, Kettlehome's secrecy, Vaerith's control, Ruk's anger, and Merrow's plan.

Sella says, "That was uncomfortable enough to be useful."

Record the trust: Kettlehome chooses action with open eyes.

Go to 38.

37. The Dragon's Price

Vaerith admits the truth: echo-iron veins run beneath Kettlehome. They store sound, strengthen repeated patterns, and could make a binding last long after singers tire.

"I hid the ore because every kingdom that learns of it will arrive carrying contracts in one hand and soldiers in the other," he says.

He is not wrong. He is not innocent.

Record the clue: echo-iron is why Merrow wants the mountain and why Vaerith hid the valley.

If you warn Rivergate and Kettlehome together, go to 38.

If you let Vaerith solve it alone, go to 41.

38. Terms Before Battle

You bring Rivergate, Kettlehome, and Banner needs into one room or one urgent chain of messages.

The terms are ugly because they are real: land for families who disarm, mixed patrols, public trial for Merrow, no echo-iron mining, Rivergate inspection without occupation, and Vaerith giving up unilateral control of entry.

No one likes all of it. That is how you know it might work.

Record the progress: a treaty framework exists before the final crisis.

Go to 42.

39. Witnesses Alive

You get students, clerks, and Master Gongo out alive. Some notes burn. A few instruments are smashed. But living witnesses can answer questions that paper cannot.

Gongo coughs smoke and says, "The counter-pattern needs different voices. Not one perfect singer. Different voices."

Record the clue: many different voices can break a single controlling pattern.

Go to 42.

40. Documents Saved, People Missing

You save ledgers, contracts, and binding notes, but several witnesses vanish in the attack.

The law will have paper. It will not have every voice. Merrow's trial becomes possible, but colder and easier for powerful people to shape.

Record the consequence: evidence is strong, but public trust is weaker.

Go to 43.

41. Ending - The Dragon Solves It

You let Vaerith act alone.

He finds Merrow's agents in the lower caves and scatters them with fire, fear, and old magic. The immediate threat ends. Rivergate receives a warning it cannot ignore. The Banner flees deeper into the hills.

Kettlehome survives, but the old bargain tightens. Vaerith is praised for saving everyone from a danger made worse by secrets he kept.

This is a survival ending. The valley is safe for now, but the central problem remains.

42. The Echo-Iron Caves

You enter the listening caves with allies who do not fully trust one another.

That is good. Merrow's binding depends on one voice repeated perfectly. Your answer depends on difference.

At the central platform, Merrow stands between iron ribs while hired chanters repeat Vaerith's hidden name. Green light binds the dragon's wings and throat.

Kettlehome work rhythm, Rivergate testimony, Banner mourning, courier rhymes, watch commands, college technique, and one dragon's furious bass collide inside the iron ribs.

Merrow's binding depends on ownership. Your song has none.

The green chains break.

Go to 44.

43. Ending - Evidence Without Chorus

Merrow is exposed and Vaerith is freed later, but the story reaches the public in fragments. Rivergate trusts paper more than witnesses. Kettlehome trusts secrecy more than law. The Banner trusts land more than promises.

No war begins that day. That matters. But every unresolved question waits at the next table.

This is the unfinished ending: the mystery is solved, but the campaign continues.

44. Ending - Treaty at Dawn

At dawn, no one receives everything.

Rivergate recognizes Kettlehome's boundaries and guest-law. Kettlehome opens one trade road. Black Banner families who surrender raiding claims receive terrace land and legal standing. Mixed patrols answer to one Rivergate officer and one Kettlehome warden. Echo-iron extraction is forbidden for ten years. Vaerith gives up unilateral control of entry to the valley.

Merrow faces public trial. Her grief is recorded. So are her crimes.

This is the best ending: not clean, not simple, but honest enough to last.
