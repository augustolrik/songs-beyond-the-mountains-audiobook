Songs Beyond the Mountains - D&D Campaign Guide

This guide turns the novel material into a tabletop campaign framework. It is written so a Dungeon Master can run the setting before, during, after, or beside the events of the book without needing to copy the novel scene by scene.

The central campaign question:

What happens when a hidden goblin valley, a fearful human city, displaced goblin refugees, a controlling copper dragon, and a ruthless merchant house all want safety - but define safety in incompatible ways?

Campaign Tone

Use this as a political mystery, road investigation, and cultural bridge-building campaign.

  • Primary mode: investigation, diplomacy, travel, rescue, faction pressure.
  • Secondary mode: dungeon exploration through echoing caves, ambushes on trade roads, sabotage, social duels, courtroom testimony.
  • Avoid making goblins "the monster faction." The strongest version of this campaign works when every group has understandable fears and bad incentives.
  • Violence should have consequences. Winning a fight may make peace harder.
  • Songs, names, memories, and public stories matter as much as swords.

Recommended Level Range

Short campaign

Levels 3-6, about 8-12 sessions.

Start with the burned caravans and end with the exposure of Lady Merrow or the rescue in the echo-iron caves.

Full campaign

Levels 1-8 or 3-10, about 18-30 sessions.

Start with border rumors and low-level road work. Build toward the Black Banner, echo-iron, Vaerith's secrets, Merrow's conspiracy, and the treaty at dawn.

High-level sequel

Levels 8-12.

Set after the treaty. The valley is known, echo-iron is forbidden, Merrow's allies still have money, and other powers want the mountain's stored voices.

Core Premise for Player Characters

The player characters are drawn into a fragile peace process between Rivergate and the hidden goblin valley of Kettlehome. Caravan attacks have been staged to look like goblin raids. A human merchant, Lady Merrow Vale, is secretly financing violence to gain control of echo-iron, a resonant ore that can store and repeat sound, song, commands, and magical patterns.

The truth is complicated:

  • Kettlehome has been hidden for generations under the protection and control of Vaerith, a copper dragon.
  • Rivergate fears goblin raids and dragon power, but it also profits from trade.
  • The Black Banner is a real goblin militant faction, but many of its members are displaced families looking for safety.
  • Lady Merrow is guilty, but she exploits real fears.
  • Vaerith protects Kettlehome, but he also censors maps, controls movement, and hides the value of echo-iron.
  • Gobolin's mission is not just to prove innocence. It is to replace secrecy with a dangerous but necessary public truth.

Player Character Entry Points

Choose one or combine several.

Rivergate hires the party

The council hires the party to investigate attacks on the trade road. They are told goblins may be responsible, but the evidence is suspiciously neat.

Best for: classic adventuring party, urban investigation, mixed social/combat play.

Kettlehome sends the party

The characters are goblins, allies of Kettlehome, or outsiders trusted by Vaerith. They must reach Rivergate, survive prejudice, and prove the valley does not want war.

Best for: goblin-centered campaign, diplomacy, identity, family politics.

The party travels with Gobolin

Gobolin is an NPC envoy. The player characters are guards, witnesses, translators, couriers, musicians, or companions assigned to keep him alive.

Best for: running close to the novel while letting players make their own choices.

The party replaces Gobolin

The characters carry the treaty-ring, trade offer, or hidden map themselves. Gobolin may exist as a bardic student, cousin, or later ally.

Best for: maximum player agency.

After the treaty

The treaty is signed, but peace is fragile. The party works for the joint council, tracks Merrow's remaining assets, protects Black Banner families, and investigates illegal echo-iron buyers.

Best for: sequel play and sandbox politics.

Major Factions

Kettlehome

Hidden goblin valley of terraces, red roofs, bathhouses, copperwork, songs, and practical clan politics.

Public face

A secret goblin settlement protected by an ancient dragon.

Inner truth

Kettlehome is beautiful, disciplined, frightened, and dependent on a bargain many residents did not personally choose.

Wants

  • Recognition of valley borders.
  • Safe trade with Rivergate.
  • Protection from anti-goblin violence.
  • Control over who enters the valley.
  • A future not dictated entirely by Vaerith.

Pressure points

  • Fear of exposure.
  • Fear of Rivergate law.
  • Internal division over Vaerith.
  • Guilt over goblins outside the valley who were not protected.
  • Dependence on secrecy and omitted maps.

Adventure uses

  • Clan council debate.
  • Missing trader from Kettlehome.
  • Secret path discovered by outsiders.
  • Young goblins wanting to leave.
  • Old goblins wanting to close the valley forever.
  • Sabotage against the first mixed patrol.

Rivergate

Human trade city with walls, guilds, patrols, a lower ward, noble families, and a council that often confuses order with justice.

Public face

A threatened city trying to keep its roads safe.

Inner truth

Rivergate is not one opinion. It contains frightened farmers, ambitious merchants, honest guards, grieving families, exploited workers, cautious priests, and people who have never met a goblin but know exactly what they think of them.

Wants

  • Safe roads.
  • Trade control.
  • Legal authority over border violence.
  • Protection from dragons.
  • Political stability.

Pressure points

  • Merchant money shapes policy.
  • Public fear can outrun evidence.
  • Lower ward losses are counted last.
  • Nobles can hide crimes behind contracts.
  • Law is tested when the accused has title and wealth.

Adventure uses

  • Council hearings.
  • Warehouse infiltration.
  • Patrol escort.
  • Mob tension at the gate.
  • Trial of Lady Merrow.
  • Lower ward witnesses who know more than officials.

Vaerith of the Verdigris Crown

Copper dragon, protector, censor, patron, jailer, and political problem.

Public face

The dragon who keeps Kettlehome safe.

Inner truth

Vaerith loves being necessary. He protects Kettlehome, but he has also controlled maps, roads, rumors, and information. His protection is real. So is the cost.

Wants

  • Kettlehome alive.
  • Echo-iron hidden.
  • Rivergate kept at a manageable distance.
  • His own cleverness recognized.
  • No one else controlling the mountain.

Pressure points

  • Pride.
  • Fear of losing control.
  • Secret guilt over what he has hidden.
  • Underestimation of short-lived people.
  • Attachment to Kettlehome that he expresses badly.

Adventure uses

  • Patron with withheld information.
  • Overpowered ally who makes diplomacy harder.
  • Bound dragon rescue set piece.
  • Negotiation over limits on dragon authority.
  • Moral question: can protection become possession?

The Black Banner

Militant goblin faction led by Ruk and supported by displaced goblin families.

Public face

Raiders, rebels, and caravan attackers.

Inner truth

The Banner includes fighters, refugees, ration keepers, elders, and children. Some members committed crimes. Others followed the only leader who promised land, papers, and safety.

Wants

  • A home.
  • End of bounty hunting and forced wandering.
  • Leverage against humans and Vaerith.
  • Recognition that Kettlehome's safety was not shared with all goblins.

Pressure points

  • Ruk needs victory to justify losses.
  • Scrip and other hardliners may prefer revenge.
  • Mother Tekk and Korr represent survival, not glory.
  • Merrow's betrayal can split the faction.
  • Many members will surrender if offered land and legal safety.

Adventure uses

  • Enemy faction that can become ally.
  • Camp diplomacy.
  • Rescue of noncombatants.
  • Duel of legitimacy between Ruk and Gobolin.
  • Hard choice between arrest, amnesty, and land settlement.

Lady Merrow Vale and the Consortium

Merchant noble, strategist, conspirator, and villain whose fear has been refined into policy.

Public face

A cautious merchant protecting Rivergate from goblins and dragons.

Inner truth

Merrow lost family to dragonfire and believes private control is the only real safeguard. She uses grief to justify murder, staged attacks, illegal mining, and magical coercion.

Wants

  • Control of echo-iron.
  • Exclusive access to Kettlehome's trade.
  • Rivergate dependence on her logistics.
  • Vaerith bound or neutralized.
  • Public fear directed toward goblins.

Pressure points

  • She needs plausible deniability.
  • She relies on ledgers, contracts, hired specialists, and shell partnerships.
  • She underestimates people she treats as tools.
  • Her story works only while goblins remain faceless.
  • Her grief is sincere, but sincerity does not reduce guilt.

Adventure uses

  • Patron who is secretly villain.
  • Legal opponent who cannot be defeated by suspicion alone.
  • Warehouse evidence trail.
  • Social encounter in winter garden.
  • Trial or escape arc.

Important NPCs

Gobolin Hearthstone

Goblin envoy, musician, bridge-builder, and reluctant political symbol.

Motive

He wants Kettlehome recognized and wants to study music at the College of the Golden Harp. He also wants to stop being treated as a treaty with legs.

Useful at the table

Use him as an NPC if the party needs a diplomatic center. Do not let him solve everything. He asks questions, makes risky speeches, and notices when a story is too convenient.

Secrets and tensions

  • He carries Vaerith's treaty-ring.
  • He wants more freedom from the dragon than he admits at first.
  • Ruk is his cousin.
  • He is tempted to copy Vaerith's habit of using technically accurate lies.

Pip Thistledown

Halfling courier with local routes, coded pouches, practical courage, and sharp class instincts.

Motive

She wants the people ignored by official power to be counted. She knows that records, routes, and delivery marks can expose lies.

Useful at the table

She can provide rumors, maps, witness names, hidden paths, and lower ward contacts.

Edric Vale

Rivergate patrolman wounded in the opening attacks. Honest, stubborn, and connected to the city without being powerful inside it.

Motive

He wants Rivergate protected without letting fear make it cruel.

Useful at the table

He gives the party guard access, patrol context, and a human witness willing to complicate the official story.

Moss

Half-orc musician with a broken tusk, low voice, disciplined silence, and direct experience with being misread as a threat.

Motive

She wants truth without performance. She notices rhythm, discipline, and what violence sounds like when it is trained rather than desperate.

Useful at the table

She can identify Black Banner tactics, musical clues, and echo-iron resonance. She is also a strong moral mirror for how the party treats feared outsiders.

Duchess Elira

Young ruler of Rivergate, careful, pressured, and more perceptive than many councilors believe.

Motive

She wants peace that can survive public scrutiny.

Useful at the table

She can authorize investigations, delay military action, and force legal process - but she needs evidence.

Captain Tern and Sergeant Nessa

Rivergate watch leaders. Tern is tired and practical. Nessa is controlled, precise, and attentive to the cost of commands.

Motive

They want order without becoming Merrow's private weapon.

Useful at the table

They can become allies if the party respects procedure and protects civilians.

Master Gongo

Goblin music scholar at the College of the Golden Harp, blunt, proud, and essential to understanding echo-iron.

Motive

He wants musical truth, not courtly approval. He also wants Gobolin to earn study honestly.

Useful at the table

He explains echo-iron, tests musical solutions, and can be kidnapped to force the party into rescue mode.

Ruk

Gobolin's cousin, Black Banner leader, wounded idealist, and dangerous survivor.

Motive

He wants a place where goblin children can sleep without sentries. He believes Vaerith's protection of Kettlehome abandoned everyone outside it.

Useful at the table

He should not be a simple villain. He is a rival leader whose accusations are often true, even when his choices are destructive.

Scrip

Black Banner archer, hardliner, sharp with grief and loyalty.

Motive

She wants revenge and proof that compromise does not simply mean surrender.

Useful at the table

She can escalate a negotiation, challenge amnesty, or become a difficult later ally.

Mother Tekk and Korr

Black Banner elder and ration keeper.

Motive

They care about survival more than speeches.

Useful at the table

They are the best way to show that the Banner is a community, not only a fighting force.

Core Locations

Kettlehome

Hidden goblin valley of terraces, copper craft, bathhouses, clan gates, songs, ledgers, and practical beauty.

Use at the table

  • Let the players see ordinary life before asking them to defend it.
  • Use clan councils as social encounters with real stakes.
  • Show that secrecy protected the valley and also narrowed its future.

Scene ideas

  • A song at the gate returns from the mountain changed.
  • A council floor divided by chalk lines.
  • Vaerith's copper ravens report private conversations.
  • Children ask whether humans really eat goblins.
  • A mixed patrol accidentally violates guest-law.

Rivergate

Walled trade city where fear, law, grief, and commerce collide.

Use at the table

  • Treat Rivergate as many neighborhoods, not one hostile crowd.
  • Give the party human allies early.
  • Make evidence matter. Rumor should not be enough to convict Merrow.

Scene ideas

  • Public oath at the gate.
  • The Bent Nail as a rumor hub.
  • Council chamber negotiations.
  • Winter garden meeting with Merrow.
  • East quay warehouse search.
  • Lower ward families naming missing drivers.

The Trade Road

The contested route between Rivergate and the mountains.

Use at the table

  • Investigation site.
  • Ambush route.
  • Refugee crossing.
  • Symbol of whether peace is real.

Clues to place

  • Wrong goblin knots.
  • Wagon tracks matching Merrow freight.
  • Black cloth with dye Kettlehome does not use.
  • Contracts hidden as agricultural tools.
  • Survivors who heard disciplined commands, not chaotic raiding.

The College of the Golden Harp

Music college in Rivergate. Social gatekeeping, real expertise, and magical resonance research meet here.

Use at the table

  • Let bard, wizard, artificer, cleric, and scholar characters shine.
  • Make performance an investigation tool, not only entertainment.
  • Use the attack on the college as a major midpoint escalation.

Scene ideas

  • Audition before skeptical masters.
  • Echo-iron demonstration.
  • Debate over whether goblin music is scholarship.
  • Night of Broken Strings attack.
  • Rescue of kidnapped teachers or students.

The Listening Caves

Caverns beneath the mountains where echo-iron veins preserve sound and memory.

Use at the table

  • Environmental dungeon.
  • Puzzle site.
  • Memory archive.
  • Battlefield where sound changes magic.

Hazards

  • Repeated phrases triggering old commands.
  • Stealth checks complicated by returning echoes.
  • Illusions made from stored memories.
  • Combat rhythms that strengthen or weaken spells.
  • Areas where lies return in the speaker's own voice.

Echo-Iron

Resonant ore that stores and reinforces patterned vibration.

What it can do

  • Preserve voices, songs, commands, and emotional echoes.
  • Sustain a magical pattern longer than a living caster could.
  • Reinforce a binding, alarm, charm, or ward.
  • Reveal old memories if struck, sung to, or magically tuned.

What it cannot do

  • Invent new commands.
  • Understand meaning.
  • Decide who is right.
  • Replace living consent.

Campaign danger

Echo-iron turns repetition into power. That makes it dangerous in politics as well as magic. Propaganda, legal oaths, binding names, work songs, funeral lines, and military commands can all become magical architecture.

Sample mechanics

Use simple table rules unless you want a full subsystem.

  • A character proficient with Performance, Arcana, History, or a relevant instrument can identify a stored pattern.
  • Repeating a known counter-rhythm can suppress an echo-iron effect for 1 minute.
  • A single repeated voice strengthens control magic.
  • A chorus of different voices can disrupt control magic.
  • Thunder damage, shatter, silence, calm emotions, dispel magic, and bardic music should all interact with echo-iron in interesting ways.

Campaign Structure

Act I - The Envoy and the City

Goal

Get the party from suspicion to provisional trust.

Major beats

  1. Road attack or wounded patrol.
  2. First goblin evidence that looks too perfect.
  3. Arrival at Rivergate under public suspicion.
  4. The Bent Nail rumor web.
  5. Council hearing and limited investigation window.
  6. College of the Golden Harp introduces echo-iron.

Key choice

Do the players treat Rivergate as an enemy, a court, or a community?

Possible sessions

  • Session 1: Wounded patrol and staged clue.
  • Session 2: Gate oath and first social pressure.
  • Session 3: Tavern rumors and faction contacts.
  • Session 4: Council hearing.
  • Session 5: College test and echo-iron clue.

Act II - The Broken Accord

Goal

Expose the staged conflict before war becomes useful to too many people.

Major beats

  1. Burned caravan investigation.
  2. Captured raider reveals Black Banner and paid violence.
  3. Evidence points toward Merrow but is not yet enough.
  4. Vaerith reveals the echo-iron secret.
  5. The college is attacked.
  6. The party chooses rescue, law, diplomacy, or flight.

Key choice

Do the players expose every truth, including Vaerith's secrets, or only the truths useful to their side?

Possible sessions

  • Session 6: Caravan ash field.
  • Session 7: Bridge ambush and captured raider.
  • Session 8: Warehouse and customs records.
  • Session 9: Merrow social encounter.
  • Session 10: Vaerith's withheld truth.
  • Session 11: Attack on the college.
  • Session 12: Outlaw rescue planning.

Act III - Songs Beyond the Mountains

Goal

Resolve the conflict without letting Merrow, Vaerith, Ruk, or Rivergate own the whole truth.

Major beats

  1. Kettlehome's internal split.
  2. Listening cave exploration.
  3. Black Banner confrontation.
  4. Merrow binds Vaerith with echo-iron and stolen song-magic.
  5. The party disrupts the binding through many voices.
  6. Treaty negotiations decide what peace costs.

Key choice

Can the players build a peace that includes the people who were easiest to blame?

Possible sessions

  • Session 13: Return to Kettlehome.
  • Session 14: Listening caves.
  • Session 15: Black Banner camp.
  • Session 16: Dragon in Chains.
  • Session 17: The Song Without an Owner.
  • Session 18: Treaty at Dawn.

Plot Hooks

Road and Investigation Hooks

  • A caravan is destroyed, but the goblin knots on the evidence are tied the wrong way.
  • A wounded patrolman insists his rescuer was a goblin.
  • A courier carries names of missing drivers and asks the party to make sure the council hears them.
  • A merchant house pays unusually well for escort work along a route it claims is too dangerous.
  • A Black Banner raider dies asking the party to tell Ruk that "the terms were broken."
  • A bridge repeats a command in a voice no one nearby is using.

Rivergate Hooks

  • The party is hired to protect a council hearing from unrest.
  • A lower ward family asks the party to find a missing driver whose death was reduced to a cargo line.
  • A clerk notices warehouse weights that do not match the declared goods.
  • A temple singer disappears after accepting private work from Merrow.
  • The Bent Nail's musician hears the same rhythm in three unrelated attacks.
  • The duchess needs outside investigators because every local guard is politically compromised.

Kettlehome Hooks

  • Vaerith's ravens begin repeating conversations they should not have heard.
  • A young goblin wants to leave the valley and asks the party for protection.
  • A clan council accuses Vaerith of hiding more than roads.
  • Someone opens an old sealed passage from the wrong side.
  • Echoes from the mountain repeat a forgotten warning in a dead elder's voice.
  • Black Banner relatives ask Kettlehome why their children were not worth saving.

Black Banner Hooks

  • A Banner camp contains more children and elders than fighters.
  • Scrip challenges the party to prove the treaty will feed anyone.
  • Mother Tekk offers information in exchange for medicine and safe land.
  • Korr's ration slate proves Merrow counted Banner families as disposable labor.
  • Ruk asks why Rivergate law never walked to Blackfen.
  • A splinter group wants to kill Merrow before trial, risking the whole peace.

Echo-Iron Hooks

  • A miner hears his own voice giving orders he never spoke.
  • A noble buys broken instruments from the college.
  • A cave keeps repeating the last argument held there until visitors join the rhythm.
  • A legal oath spoken near echo-iron becomes magically binding by accident.
  • A child sings a valley song in Rivergate despite never hearing it before.
  • A foreign power offers to buy "singing ore" at any price.

Mysteries and Secrets

Use these as layered reveals.

Early reveals

  • The caravan evidence is staged.
  • The attackers are disciplined, not starving bandits.
  • Rivergate contains allies as well as bigots.
  • Kettlehome goblins are not the same as the raiders.
  • Merrow has prepared warehouses and freight alternatives before the attacks justify them.

Mid-campaign reveals

  • The Black Banner is real, but Merrow is paying and manipulating parts of it.
  • Ruk forbade killing children, but Merrow's people broke the terms.
  • Echo-iron is the real strategic prize.
  • Vaerith has burned maps and controlled access to knowledge.
  • The College of the Golden Harp knows more about sound magic than it admits.

Late reveals

  • Merrow wants to bind Vaerith, not merely expose him.
  • Echo-iron can maintain a binding if taught the right repeated pattern.
  • The solution is not one perfect voice but many voices refusing to match.
  • Peace requires public truth about Merrow, Vaerith, Rivergate law, Kettlehome secrecy, and Black Banner displacement.

| Edric | Lawful protection | Rivergate law that reaches beyond walls | His city becoming what it claims to fight |

Adventure Design Principles

Make clues point in more than one direction

A wrong goblin knot should not simply say "humans did it." It should say:

  • Someone wanted goblins blamed.
  • The copyist knew some goblin customs but not enough.
  • A goblin insider may still be involved.
  • The truth is staged, but the danger is real.

Let social encounters change the map

Winning an argument should open routes, delay troops, protect witnesses, or change who attends the treaty. Losing one should not end the campaign, but it should make the next scene harder.

Make prejudice mechanically visible

Do not rely only on speeches. Show it through:

  • Higher prices.
  • Guards asking the wrong person to explain.
  • Witnesses refusing to speak in public.
  • Councilors accepting weak evidence against goblins but demanding perfect evidence against nobles.
  • Inns deciding who gets a room by "available space."

Give every faction a sympathetic witness

  • Kettlehome: Sella, Old Brik, children at the gate.
  • Rivergate: Edric, lower ward families, Duchess Elira.
  • Black Banner: Mother Tekk, Korr, displaced children.
  • Vaerith's side: people alive because his secrecy worked.
  • Merrow's fear: a record of the dragon attack that killed Alric.

Do not make peace cheap

A good ending should still require concessions:

  • Rivergate recognizes Kettlehome boundaries.
  • Kettlehome opens one trade road.
  • Black Banner families receive land if they surrender raiding claims.
  • Echo-iron extraction is forbidden for ten years.
  • Mixed patrols require joint command.
  • Vaerith gives up unilateral authority over entry to the valley.
  • Merrow faces law, not private revenge.

Session Tools

Rumor Table

Roll or choose.

  1. "The goblin envoy carries a dragon's mouth around his neck."
  2. "Merrow bought warehouses before the second caravan burned."
  3. "The Bent Nail serves anyone who pays, which is why decent people avoid it."
  4. "The Black Banner paint white hands over their mouths so the dead can speak through them."
  5. "The mountain repeats songs if it likes them."
  6. "Vaerith can hear through copper."
  7. "A Rivergate patrolman says a goblin saved his life."
  8. "The college has a locked room where broken bells keep ringing."
  9. "The lower ward lost more drivers than the council records admit."
  10. "Ruk was born in Kettlehome and left angry."
  11. "Merrow's brother died in dragonfire."
  12. "Some goblins outside the valley call Kettlehome a golden cage."

Evidence Table

Use these as clues or handouts.

  1. Wrongly tied Kettlehome cord.
  2. Black cloth dyed with oak gall and iron, not Kettlehome soot-black.
  3. Wagon tracks too wide for hill carts.
  4. Customs copies showing ore-cart weights under false labels.
  5. Ledger phrase: "unencumbered guarantor."
  6. Broken pearl button from Merrow's glove.
  7. Echo-iron sliver that repeats one command.
  8. Ration slate listing Black Banner families.
  9. College notes on binding names.
  10. Burned map fragment with Vaerith's claw mark.

Noncombat Encounter Ideas

  • Convince a frightened innkeeper to house goblin witnesses.
  • Translate a Kettlehome guest-law into Rivergate legal language.
  • Cross-examine a merchant clerk without exposing the informant.
  • Teach a mixed group a counter-rhythm before the cave repeats a harmful spell.
  • Help Ruk and a grieving Rivergate family sit at the same table.
  • Negotiate what weapons mixed patrols leave at the border.

Combat Encounter Ideas

Use combat when choices fail, secrets break, or someone tries to silence witnesses.

  • Ambush at a burned caravan site.
  • Bridge fight where echo-iron repeats a command every round.
  • Warehouse guards using nonlethal orders to delay discovery.
  • College attackers cutting strings, bells, and throats of evidence.
  • Black Banner hardliners testing whether the party came to talk or arrest.
  • Final platform fight while Merrow's binding song grows stronger.

Echo-Iron Encounter Rules

Optional simple rules:

  • At initiative count 20, the echo-iron repeats the strongest patterned sound from the previous round.
  • If the repeated sound was a command, creatures who heard the original must make a Wisdom save or hesitate, move, or repeat an action in a limited way.
  • A character can use an action and a successful Performance, Arcana, or relevant tool check to disrupt the rhythm.
  • If at least three characters contribute different rhythms, songs, prayers, or shouted names, the binding effect weakens.
  • Silence suppresses local echo-iron but may also prevent the party's counter-song.
  • Thunder damage overloads a small vein but may release stored memories unpredictably.

Campaign Endings

Public Treaty Ending

The party exposes Merrow, saves Vaerith without restoring his old authority, brings Ruk's people into negotiation, and helps Rivergate and Kettlehome sign a public treaty.

Best if the players gathered evidence, protected witnesses, and resisted easy scapegoats.

Dragon's Bargain Ending

Vaerith is freed and Merrow is defeated, but the dragon reasserts control over Kettlehome "for safety."

Useful if the party relied on Vaerith too often or hid his secrets.

Rivergate Security Ending

Merrow falls, but Rivergate demands inspection, military oversight, or exclusive trade rights over the valley.

Useful if the party exposed goblin violence but failed to humanize Kettlehome and the Banner.

Banner Victory Ending

Ruk survives as a major power. The Banner gets land and leverage, but trust remains thin.

Useful if the party prioritized displaced goblins over formal law.

Broken Peace Ending

War is avoided for now, but Merrow escapes, echo-iron samples vanish, or the treaty lacks public consent.

Useful as a sequel hook.

Sequel Seeds

  • Merrow's trial reveals buyers from another kingdom.
  • Echo-iron samples were smuggled out before extraction was banned.
  • Vaerith's black remembering stone begins speaking in a voice older than Kettlehome.
  • A mixed patrol vanishes near Blackfen.
  • Ruk's new terraces are sabotaged.
  • The College of the Golden Harp splits over whether echo-iron research should continue.
  • A human village asks to join Kettlehome guest-law after Rivergate fails them.
  • The mountain repeats a song no living person admits knowing.
  • Scrip disappears with a hardline faction.
  • Gobolin returns from a journey carrying a treaty from a place no map names.

Quick Start: First Three Sessions

Session 1 - The Road That Watches

Open with a wounded Rivergate patrolman and staged evidence near a ruined roadside shelter. Let the players save someone before asking who they believe.

End with a choice: go to Rivergate publicly, follow the attackers' trail, or seek Kettlehome's hidden road.

Session 2 - At the Gates

Run Rivergate's gate as a public social encounter. The party must prevent panic, establish provisional trust, and decide whether to reveal a goblin connection immediately.

End with the party receiving a limited investigation window.

Session 3 - The Bent Nail

The party gathers rumors at an unfashionable inn. Introduce Pip, Moss, Edric, and conflicting theories. Drop the first Merrow warehouse clue.

End with either a council summons, a warehouse lead, or a Black Banner message.

DM Notes on Adapting the Novel

You do not need to preserve the novel's exact sequence. Preserve the pressure:

  • A hidden people wants recognition.
  • A city wants safety.
  • A dragon wants control.
  • A displaced faction wants a home.
  • A merchant villain weaponizes fear.
  • A magical resource makes every political lie more dangerous.
  • The best solution requires many voices instead of one perfect hero.

If the player characters replace novel characters, keep the original cast as witnesses, rivals, patrons, and consequences. If the player characters travel beside the novel characters, make sure the players decide the hard parts: who to trust, what to reveal, whom to forgive, and what peace costs.
